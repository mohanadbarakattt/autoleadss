## 1. FELICITY PROPERTIES — felicitypro.com

### The problem you named

"There's a lot of info and different projects, I don't know what to do for him."

That is not your problem to solve creatively — it is **his positioning problem**,
and the site is the evidence. It currently sells eight services (property tours,
market analysis, home staging, property management, investment solutions, buyer
representation, property marketing, relocation) across four projects. Most of
that is Durable template padding. It reads as a brokerage that does everything,
which is the same as a brokerage that does nothing in particular.

### What he actually has [verified]

One sentence in his CEO bio is the entire business:

> "...access to allocations before they reach the portals."

That is the **only** thing a Dubai buyer cannot get from Bayut, Property Finder
or Dubizzle. Listings are commodity. Market analysis is commodity. Early
allocation access is not.

Two more facts that matter:

- **Three of his four listed projects are Dubai Creek Harbour** — Altan, Albero,
  Silva (all Emaar). The fourth is DAMAC Islands 2 in Dubailand. He already has a
  de facto niche and isn't claiming it.
- The site footer already says: *"Get my weekly Dubai market brief on WhatsApp —
  join 100+ investors."* **He has already started the right machine.** That list
  is worth more than the entire website.

Also verified: RERA ORN 33951 · 20+ developer partners incl. Emaar, DAMAC, Sobha,
Aldar · serves buyers in 12+ countries · WhatsApp +971 56 352 0611 · Office 705,
Building 12, Bay Square · runs Reelly (off-plan broker data platform) · blog is
active (posts 25/28/31 Jul 2026).

### The strategy

**Stop selling "Dubai real estate." Sell Creek Harbour allocations, via WhatsApp.**

Narrow to one community, one offer, one capture mechanism. Everything else is
noise, and the noise is exactly what made you feel stuck.

### Website rebuild

Not a bigger site — a smaller one.

- Kill the eight generic service blocks. They are template filler and they dilute.
- Hero: Muhannad's face + the allocation promise. Real estate sells through a
  person, not a logo.
- **One landing page per project**, not a listings grid. Each carries: payment
  plan, handover date, entry price, floor plans, community.
- Primary CTA everywhere: **join the WhatsApp brief.** Secondary: book a call.
- Add Arabic. [assumption: also worth testing Russian — Dubai off-plan buyer mix.
  Confirm with him which nationalities actually convert before building it.]
- Get off Durable. He is selling AED 1M+ property on a template builder, and in
  real estate the product *is* trust.

**Bug to fix on day one:** the testimonial carousel repeats the same three
testimonials three times. It reads as padding. [verified]

### Ads

One campaign per project. Never "Dubai real estate."

- Test Altan vs Albero vs Silva against each other — same audience, different
  creative, let the project pick itself.
- Audiences: UAE residents, plus the countries he already sells into.
  [assumption — ask him which 3–4 countries actually close, don't guess.]
- Creative angles: payment plan, handover date, "what AED X gets you in Creek
  Harbour", ROI.
- **Conversion event is the WhatsApp brief opt-in, not "contact us."** Off-plan
  has a long consideration cycle; the list is where the deal actually happens.

### Posts (4/day)

Muhannad on camera. Pillars:

1. Project breakdowns — one building at a time
2. Payment plan explainers (the single most-searched off-plan question)
3. "What AED X buys you in ___" comparisons
4. Handover and market news
5. Myth-busting for first-time off-plan buyers

English + Arabic versions of each.

### Chatbot

Qualifies on four things: **budget · cash vs payment plan · investor vs end-user ·
country of residence.** Then routes — hot straight to Muhannad's WhatsApp,
everyone else onto the brief list.

The real value is timezone coverage: he sells into 12+ countries, so leads arrive
at 2am Dubai time and currently wait until morning.

### Build this first

**One complete funnel for one project.** Recommend Albero or Silva — Creek
Harbour, 1–3 bedrooms, lowest entry price, therefore widest audience.

Landing page + 3 ad creatives + the WhatsApp opt-in flow. Something he can switch
on, not a mockup.

## 2. SEREEN CARE — sereencare.ae

**Deal: work in exchange for an ad to his audience.** So the deliverable must be
*shareable* — the thing he posts should double as the proof of your work.

### Their site is genuinely good [verified]

Better than expected. Sharp positioning ("Professional Cleaning With Proof, Not
Promises"), a defined four-step process, a competitor comparison table, and a real
guarantee (report within 24h, return visit within 48h, no charge). Operating since
1991. Four services: deep cleaning, post-renovation, move in/out, pest control —
plus a combined cleaning+pest single-visit upsell.

Do not rewrite this. Whoever wrote it knew what they were doing.

### The gaps

1. **Their entire promise is "proof" and there is not one before/after photo on
   the homepage.** The comparison table literally claims "Before and after photos
   provided" — and shows none. This is the biggest single miss across all three
   clients, and it is free to fix.
2. **No Arabic — based in Sharjah.** The most Arabic-majority emirate. Their own
   local market can't read their site.
3. **One testimonial**, for a company operating since 1991. The one they do have
   is from a property manager — exactly the right buyer — and it's alone.
4. **No pricing anywhere.** Cleaning is price-shopped. "Request a quote" loses to
   a competitor showing "from AED 299."
5. Zero ads.

### The real money is B2B [assumption — confirm with him]

The site already talks to landlords, property managers, tenancy handovers,
commercial spaces, before-handover. The single testimonial is a property manager
who says he uses them "for every unit turnover."

That is recurring contract revenue, and nothing on the site goes after it
specifically. Consumer one-off cleans are the low-margin distraction.

### Website rebuild

- **Before/after gallery as the hero.** Their differentiator, currently invisible.
- Add Arabic.
- Add price ranges ("from AED ___") to kill top-of-funnel friction.
- Add a dedicated **property manager / B2B page** — contracts, volume pricing,
  unit turnover, SLAs.
- Surface "since 1991" and the square-footage counter properly. 34 years is an
  enormous trust asset in a category full of fly-by-night operators.
- Collect and publish more testimonials. They must exist.

### Ads

Two tracks:

- **Consumer intent** — move-out cleaning, post-renovation, pest. Geo Dubai +
  Sharjah. Arabic *and* English.
- **B2B** — property managers, landlords, holiday-home operators. LinkedIn + Meta.

Creative is before/after. Always. It is the highest-performing format in this
category and they already own the assets.

### Posts (4/day)

Before/after is the entire content engine. Plus:

- "The 7 areas every cleaning company skips" (their own positioning, as content)
- Pest identification — what you actually have and what it means
- Tenancy handover checklists (they already blog maintenance checklists)
- Post-renovation dust: why normal cleaning fails

Arabic-first, given Sharjah.

### Chatbot — the best fit of the three

Their quote process **already requires photos or a video walkthrough** before a
site visit. [verified — stated in their FAQ.]

So the bot does exactly that, on WhatsApp: property type → size → service needed →
collect photos → return a price range → book the site visit. It converts their
biggest friction point into an automated asset, and it works at 11pm when someone
is moving out tomorrow morning.

### Build this first

**One before/after reel** — shareable, which is literally the payment. Plus an
**Arabic version of the homepage hero**. Don't pitch the Arabic gap; just show it
to him. It explains itself.

## 3. KEMETRA NATURALS — kemetranaturals.com

**Fee: ~$1,500/mo.**

### The price check

At LE 350–699 per item (~$7–14) and [assumption] a 2–3 item basket, they'd need
roughly **250–500 orders a month** for $1,500 to sit at a healthy 15–20% of
revenue.

That is achievable for an established Egyptian DTC brand. **$3,500 would have
needed ~2,300 orders/month and was never realistic.** $1,500 is the right call.

### What they have [verified]

Six categories: fragrances, soaps, candles, body lotions, essential oils, reed
diffusers. Prices LE 250–699.

- **Artisan Curve trays are already sold out** — demand exists, supply is the
  constraint.
- **Camel Milk Soap (LE 499)** — distinctive, Egyptian, ownable. This is the
  signature product.
- Two sisters, backgrounds in natural ingredients, essential oils and traditional
  formulation.
- Mission: employing single mothers and women facing economic hardship — stable
  work, skill development.
- Shopify, English **and Arabic already** (the only one of the three with it).
- **No WhatsApp** — in Egypt, where WhatsApp is the default commerce channel.
- No blog. Zero ads.

### The core insight

Their most valuable asset is the story, and on the site it sits **below the fold,
as a plain text block, with no faces and no workshop.**

Two sisters building a skincare brand that employs women in hardship is genuinely
moving, and it is *true* — which matters, because we've kept invented claims off
autoleadss.com deliberately and the same standard applies to client work. Nothing
here needs exaggerating. It needs showing.

### Website rebuild

Shopify, so this is theming, not rebuilding.

- Sisters and workshop **above the fold**, with faces.
- Product photography is the whole game in this category — likely the highest-
  impact single investment.
- **Add WhatsApp.** Biggest functional gap, and in Egypt it may outperform
  checkout entirely.
- **Add bundles and gift sets.** Raising AOV is the single highest-leverage thing
  available — it is what makes them sustainably affordable as a client.
- Add a blog for organic.

### Ads

Egypt. Meta + TikTok.

- Creative: process shots — hands, pouring, curing, wrapping. Craft sells craft.
- The story as a video ad.
- **Test gift bundles hard.** Ramadan, Eid and Mother's Day are the volume moments
  in Egyptian gifting.

### Posts (4/day)

The workshop *is* the content. Making soap, pouring candles, the women at work,
ingredient origins. Camel milk soap as the signature story. Arabic-first.

### Chatbot

WhatsApp order-taking + FAQ: ingredients, skin type matching, delivery, order
status. In Egypt this is plausibly the single highest-ROI item on their list.

### Honest flag before spending their money

Their constraint is AOV and possibly **inventory** — a product is already sold
out. Running ads into a catalogue that can't fulfil wastes their budget and burns
the relationship.

**Month one is bundles + a stock conversation, not spend.** Say this to them up
front; it will read as competence, not hesitation.
